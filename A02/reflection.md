# Reflection

For this assignment I used GPT-2 to change the last word of every line in *The Snow Man*. The script hides the last word, asks GPT-2 what should come next, and picks the word at rank x. I had to filter the results so it only picks real English words, because at first it kept giving me commas, line breaks and pieces of words like "gro" and "breat".

When x is small, the poem barely changes. At P+2 and P+3 GPT-2 gives words like "snow", "rain" and "forest", which already fit the winter mood, so the poem just gets more boring. P+7 is where it starts getting weird. "Pine-trees crusted with blood" and "junipers shagged with blood" made it feel like a horror movie, which I didn't expect. It also gave me "a long long", which doesn't make sense.

The higher I went, the stranger it got. I picked P+250 because it's still readable but really absurd. My favourite lines are "have been cold a long cup" and "the listener, who listens in the melancholy". They almost sound like they mean something, and that's why they're funny. I also tried P+500, but there the words were basically random ("the frost and the par"), so it stopped being funny. I think the best results are in the middle, where the word is unexpected but could still kind of fit.

If I wanted to replace every noun instead of only the last word, I would first use spaCy to find all the nouns in the poem. Then for each noun I would give GPT-2 the text before it, go down its list of predictions, keep only the words spaCy also tags as nouns, and use the seventh one. That way the grammar stays mostly okay, like in the original N+7. I tried a version in my notebook and got lines like "Of any tree in the earth of the voice". One problem is that GPT-2 only looks at the words before the noun, not after. A model like BERT looks at both sides, so it might work better for this.
